FORECAST VERIFICATION FUNDAMENTALS:

Forecast verification is the process of comparing forecasts with corresponding observations to assess their quality. The goal is not simply to measure accuracy but to diagnose the sources of forecast error and inform users about the reliability of forecast products.

KEY DEFINITIONS:

BIAS: The systematic tendency of a forecast to overestimate or underestimate the observed quantity. A rainfall forecast with positive bias consistently predicts more rainfall than observed on average.

MEAN ABSOLUTE ERROR (MAE): Average of |F - O| across a sample. Appropriate for continuous variables with well-behaved distributions; pathological for rainfall when O ≈ 0 (division not involved, but very sensitive to dry-day errors dominating).

PERCENTAGE ERROR / MAPE: Avoided for rainfall verification because near-zero observations produce undefined or infinite percentage errors. Standard WMO practice is to use anomaly-based or percentile-based error metrics for precipitation.

ANOMALY-BASED ERROR: E = |F_anom - O_anom| where anom = value minus local climatological normal. Removes mean bias and allows comparison across seasons/regions.

PERCENTILE-BASED ERROR: Expressing each error as its rank within the historical error distribution for that location/season/lead-time stratum. This is the approach used in our bust-label construction — it avoids scale issues and directly identifies unusual errors.

RELIABILITY (CALIBRATION): A forecast system is reliable if, over many forecasts, the stated probability matches the observed frequency. E.g., among all events where the model says 70% probability, approximately 70% should actually occur. The reliability diagram plots predicted probability (x-axis) vs. observed frequency (y-axis); a perfectly calibrated system falls on the diagonal.

SHARPNESS: The tendency of a forecast to predict high probabilities (near 0 or near 1) rather than near-climatology. High sharpness is only valuable when combined with good reliability.

RESOLUTION: The ability of a forecast to discriminate between events and non-events. A forecast with good resolution assigns higher probabilities to events that actually occur.

BRIER SCORE (BS): BS = (1/N) Σ (p_i - o_i)^2 where p_i is predicted probability and o_i is the binary outcome. Lower is better. Reference (climatology) score: p(bust) × (1 - p(bust)). Brier Skill Score (BSS) measures improvement over climatology reference.

ROC-AUC (Area Under ROC Curve): Discrimination ability; probability that a randomly chosen bust event is ranked higher than a randomly chosen non-bust event. AUC = 0.5: no skill (random); AUC = 1.0: perfect. Insensitive to calibration — a model can have high AUC but poor reliability.

PR-AUC (Precision-Recall AUC): More informative than ROC-AUC under class imbalance (e.g., ~10–15% bust rate). Captures the tradeoff between precision (fraction of flagged events that actually bust) and recall (fraction of actual busts that are flagged).

ISOTONIC CALIBRATION: A non-parametric method for mapping raw model scores to calibrated probabilities by fitting a monotone step function (pool-adjacent violators algorithm). Requires a separate calibration/validation set. More flexible than Platt scaling but can overfit on small validation samples.

LEAD-TIME SKILL DEGRADATION: A universal property of NWP forecasts. Skill (measured by any standard metric) decreases as lead time increases, reflecting the chaotic nature of atmospheric dynamics and the finite predictability horizon. The rate of degradation depends on scale: large-scale circulation patterns are predictable to ~10–14 days, while mesoscale convective events may be predictable for only 1–3 days. For rainfall forecasts over India, skill for heavy-rain events falls sharply beyond Day 5.

BUST vs. STANDARD ERROR: A "forecast bust" specifically denotes an error in the tail of the error distribution — beyond the 85th–90th percentile of historical errors for a given stratum — not merely any non-zero error. By definition, using an 85th percentile threshold, approximately 15% of forecasts will be labelled busts (subject to the exact stratum composition).

SPATIAL GRADIENT FEATURES IN FORECAST VERIFICATION:

DEFINITION OF SPATIAL GRADIENT:
The spatial gradient of a forecast field measures how rapidly predicted values change across neighboring grid points. A high spatial gradient in rainfall forecasts implies that one grid cell is predicted to receive significantly more or less rain than its neighbors.

PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION:
- In regions with sharp orographic boundaries (e.g., the windward vs. leeward sides of the Western Ghats), a high spatial gradient may represent a genuine, predictable physical feature.
- However, high spatial gradients over flat terrain or at scales finer than the model's effective resolution (typically 3–5× the nominal grid spacing) often indicate numerical instabilities or poorly resolved convective triggering.
- In either case, the presence of a high spatial gradient increases point-specific uncertainty: small errors in the spatial placement of a rainfall system translate to large errors at any specific grid point.

RELATIONSHIP TO BUST RISK:
- A well-known verification challenge is the "double penalty" for nearby misses: if a forecasted rain band is displaced by 50 km from where it actually occurred, every grid point in both the forecast and the observation receives a large error, even though the meteorological event was well-predicted overall.
- Our spatial_gradient feature captures this effect: high gradient means that a small displacement error (which is common and plausible at medium range) will result in a large point error.
- This is consistent with standard forecast verification theory: the same storm predicted 1 grid box away scores catastrophically on grid-point metrics.

MESOSCALE CONVECTIVE SYSTEMS (MCS) AND CLUSTER UNCERTAINTY:
Monsoon-season rainfall in India is frequently organized into MCSs — clusters of convective cells with a total horizontal extent of 100–500 km. NWP models capture MCS organization at Day 1–3 reasonably well, but the precise location, propagation speed, and intensity degrade rapidly by Day 5–7.

High spatial_gradient in the ensemble forecast (members predicting different MCS locations) is a leading indicator of this uncertainty.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION FOR BUST PROBABILITY:
When spatial_gradient is elevated (standard deviation of neighboring grid-point forecasts > 10 mm/day), our model assigns higher bust probability, consistent with the meteorological interpretation that:
1. The forecast feature is at the margin of predictability.
2. Small systematic errors will produce large local errors.
3. Operational users should widen their planning uncertainty zone.

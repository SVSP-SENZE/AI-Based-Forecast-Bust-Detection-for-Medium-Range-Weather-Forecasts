SECTION: Active/Break Monsoon Transitions and Their Impact on Forecast Predictability

---

ACTIVE/BREAK MONSOON CYCLES:

DEFINITIONS:
- Active monsoon spell: period of enhanced rainfall over central India, associated with a well-organized monsoon trough near its climatological position, active eastward moisture flux from the Bay of Bengal. Typically 3–7 days duration.
- Break monsoon spell: period of suppressed rainfall over most of India (except some Himalayan regions), associated with northward displacement of the monsoon trough and enhanced northeast India rainfall. Duration: 2–10 days.

TRANSITION DYNAMICS:
The transition between active and break spells is modulated by:
1. The Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO): 30–60 day wave that modulates tropical convection globally. Active MJO phase 2–3 enhances Indian monsoon rainfall; phases 6–7 suppress it. MJO is predictable to approximately 3 weeks.
2. Low-frequency planetary wave forcing (quasi-biweekly oscillation, 10–20 day).
3. Intraseasonal SST (sea surface temperature) variability in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.

FORECAST PREDICTABILITY AT TRANSITION POINTS:
The most challenging periods for NWP forecasting are the TRANSITIONS between active and break spells:
- The model may predict continuation of an active spell when a break is imminent.
- Or it may predict a break when an active spell is strengthening.
- These 1–3 day "transition errors" produce very large absolute errors for specific dates and specific locations.

IMPLICATIONS FOR BUST PROBABILITY:
Transition dates systematically produce higher bust rates. Our model captures this through:
- forecast_anomaly: during transitions, the anomaly can swing from large positive to large negative quickly.
- hist_bias: if similar transition dates (same season, similar synoptic pattern) historically showed large errors, hist_bias will be elevated.
- The model does NOT explicitly detect transitions (that requires regime classification — future work) but the feature signal of transition events naturally produces elevated bust probability.

SEASONAL CONTEXT:
Our doy_sin/doy_cos features encode the day-of-year in a circular fashion, allowing the model to learn that June (onset uncertainty), September (withdrawal uncertainty), and peak monsoon July–August (maximum active/break oscillation) have systematically different error characteristics.

KEY TAKEAWAY FOR RAG EXPLANATIONS:
When a forecast is flagged as Low Reliability during monsoon season (June–September), one contributing factor is the inherent difficulty of predicting active/break transitions. This is consistent with the well-documented, published literature on monsoon intraseasonal variability and is not a failure unique to our system — it reflects a fundamental physical predictability limit of the atmosphere.

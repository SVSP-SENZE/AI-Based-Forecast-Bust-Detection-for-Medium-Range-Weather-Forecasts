MAHARASHTRA CLIMATE AND RAINFALL CLIMATOLOGY:

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:
Maharashtra (18–22°N, 72–76°E) spans the Western Ghats and the interior Deccan Plateau:
- Western Ghats (Konkan coast, windward): highest rainfall in India, 2000–5000 mm/year, driven by orographic enhancement of Southwest Monsoon moisture.
- Sahyadri mountain ridges (1000–1500 m elevation) create a strong rainfall shadow.
- Interior Maharashtra (Vidarbha, Marathwada): 600–900 mm/year, highly variable, dominated by monsoon depressions and occasional cyclonic systems.

SEASONAL RAINFALL DISTRIBUTION:
- June: 15–20% of annual total; highly variable (onset uncertainty)
- July: 25–30% of annual total; peak monsoon
- August: 20–25%
- September: 15–20%; withdrawal begins NW first
- October–May: < 5%; rabi crop moisture, winter western disturbances

EXTREME RAINFALL EVENTS:
Maharashtra has recorded some of India's most extreme single-day rainfall:
- July 26, 2005 (Mumbai): 944 mm in 24 hours — one of the highest single-day totals globally; the event was poorly predicted by NWP models (a notable historical forecast bust).
- Maharashtra Vidarbha: Frequent convective events in August–September producing localized > 200 mm/day, associated with monsoon depressions.

HISTORICAL FORECAST ERROR CONTEXT:
Rainfall in western Maharashtra shows some of the highest forecast error variability in India, partly because:
1. The Ghats orographic signal is strong but very localized — small errors in wind direction translate to large rainfall errors.
2. The monsoon trough position oscillates, producing active/break cycles where the forecast can be systematically wrong for 3–5 days.
3. Arabian Sea moisture surges (LLJ — Low Level Jet intensification) can rapidly increase Konkan coast rainfall in ways that only medium-range ensemble spread captures, not deterministic runs.

BUST-PRONE SCENARIOS FOR MAHARASHTRA:
1. Pre-monsoon season (June before onset): Model places onset 3–5 days too early or late.
2. Active-to-break transition: Model continues predicting active monsoon rainfall for 1–3 extra days.
3. Monsoon depression landfall: As described in doc_08; track error of 100–200 km displaces the heavy-rain core to a different district.
4. Arabian Sea surge: Sudden intensification of the South Asian Low-Level Jet increases moisture flux faster than the model captures in subsequent runs.

GRID RESOLUTION LIMITATIONS:
The GEFSv12 model at its native ~0.25° resolution represents the large-scale monsoon circulation well but cannot resolve:
- Individual convective cells (< 10 km scale)
- Sub-grid orographic enhancements along narrow ridge sections
- Sea-breeze interactions with coastal orography

This resolution limitation is an inherent source of forecast error that our model learns as part of the hist_bias for the Maharashtra stratum.

IMPLICATIONS FOR CONFIDENCE ASSESSMENT:
For Maharashtra forecasts with high forecast_mean (> 50 mm/day), high forecast_anomaly (> 20 mm above normal), or high spatial_gradient (indicating an orographically sensitive placement), the model correctly assigns elevated bust probability, consistent with the documented historical difficulty of verifying heavy-rain events in this region.

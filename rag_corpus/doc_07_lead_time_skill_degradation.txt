LEAD TIME AND FORECAST SKILL:

THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK:
Atmospheric predictability arises from the finite growth rate of initial condition errors. For synoptic-scale extratropical systems, skill typically halves every 2–3 days. For tropical systems including monsoon rainfall, the fundamental Lorenz predictability limit is approximately 2 weeks, but practical skill (ROC-AUC or Brier Skill Score above 0) for precipitation events falls off much faster.

EMPIRICAL LEAD-TIME SKILL GRADIENT FOR INDIA RAINFALL:
From verification studies including ECMWF ERA5 and MERRA-2 reanalysis vs. GCM forecasts:
- Day 1: ROC-AUC for heavy rainfall events typically 0.85–0.95 (high skill)
- Day 3: ROC-AUC 0.75–0.85 (moderate-high skill)  
- Day 5: ROC-AUC 0.65–0.75 (moderate skill, degrading noticeably)
- Day 7: ROC-AUC 0.55–0.65 (near-climatological skill for specific events)
- Day 10: ROC-AUC ~0.50–0.60 (minimal residual skill beyond climatology)

Note: Our XGBoost bust-probability model captures this gradient through the lead_day feature and its interactions, producing ROC-AUC values of: Day 1 = 0.983, Day 3 = 0.891, Day 5 = 0.974, Day 7 = 0.914, Day 10 = 0.923 for the bust-detection task specifically.

The non-monotonic pattern (Day 5 showing higher AUC than Day 3) reflects features of the training data and the specific stratum composition (monsoon season concentrating errors differently), not a physical reversal of predictability.

WHY LEAD TIME IS A FEATURE (NOT A MODEL SPLIT):
Rather than training separate models for each lead time, the single XGBoost model uses lead_day as a feature, allowing it to learn:
1. The average skill degradation as lead time increases.
2. Non-linear interactions between lead time and other features (e.g., the effect of high ensemble spread is more damaging at Day 7 than at Day 1, because there is less time for the forecast to self-correct through subsequent model runs).

LONG-RANGE FORECAST CONTEXT (DAYS 7–10):
At these lead times, the forecast should not be interpreted as a precise point prediction. Rather:
- The model is attempting to predict the large-scale circulation pattern (e.g., monsoon trough position, MJO phase).
- Specific rainfall amounts are highly uncertain.
- Bust probability at Day 7–10 is expected to be higher on average, and the confidence band for most grid points will tend toward Moderate or Low Reliability.
- This is correct behavior, not a model failure — it accurately reflects the inherent limits of medium-range prediction.

PRACTICAL GUIDANCE FOR USERS:
For any forecast with lead_day ≥ 7:
- The primary value is identifying large-scale wet/dry signals, not pinpoint rainfall totals.
- Bust probability naturally trends higher (15–25% range is typical at Day 7–10 even for "good" forecasts).
- If the model additionally flags high spread, high spatial gradient, or high hist_bias, these compound the lead-time uncertainty.
- Action: plan for the possibility of bust while continuing to monitor subsequent model runs for convergence.

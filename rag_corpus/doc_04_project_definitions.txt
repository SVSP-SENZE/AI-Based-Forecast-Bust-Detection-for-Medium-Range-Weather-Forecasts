PROJECT-SPECIFIC DEFINITIONS (use these when explaining our model's outputs):

FORECAST BUST (this project):
A "forecast bust" in this system is defined as: for a given variable (rainfall), grid cell, lead time, and season, the realized forecast error falls in the top 15% of the historical error distribution for that exact stratum (lead-time bucket × region group × season). The threshold is empirically derived from the GEFSv12 reforecast and IMD 0.25° gridded truth data (2000–2004, Maharashtra sub-region).

Error metric: |F - O| expressed as a percentile rank within the stratum-specific historical error distribution.
Bust threshold: 85th percentile of that stratum's historical |F - O| distribution.
Bust rate realized: 14.07% of all forecast-observation pairs in the training period are classified as busts under this definition.

BUST PROBABILITY (this project):
The calibrated output of an XGBoost gradient-boosted tree model, estimating P(bust | X) where X is the feature vector available at issue time. The model is calibrated via isotonic regression fitted on the validation fold (chronological 70/15/15 split).

CONFIDENCE BAND:
- High Reliability: bust probability < 20% — the model assigns low chance of a major miss; the forecast is expected to verify within the historical normal error range.
- Moderate Reliability: 20% ≤ bust probability < 45% — meaningful uncertainty; the forecast may or may not verify; use additional cross-checks.
- Low Reliability: bust probability ≥ 45% — model identifies this as a high-risk forecast; operational users should treat it with heightened caution and prepare contingency scenarios.

FEATURE DEFINITIONS:
- forecast_mean: ensemble mean rainfall forecast (mm/day) at the grid point for the valid time.
- forecast_spread: standard deviation of ensemble members (mm/day) at that grid point. High spread → higher uncertainty.
- forecast_range: range (max – min) of ensemble members (mm/day). Complementary to spread.
- forecast_anomaly: forecast_mean – local climatological mean for that day-of-year and location. Positive = above-normal forecast.
- spatial_gradient: standard deviation of forecast values at the 3×3 neighborhood of the grid point. High gradient → possible convective/frontal boundary nearby.
- hist_bias: mean absolute error for this lead-time/season stratum computed from strictly historical (pre-issue-date) data. Indicates known systematic difficulty.
- lead_day: lead time (1, 3, 5, 7, or 10 days). Higher lead → more uncertainty by construction.
- doy_sin, doy_cos: cyclically encoded day-of-year to capture seasonal signal without discontinuity at December/January.
- month: calendar month (1–12).

WHAT THE MODEL DOES NOT DO:
- It does not predict the direction or magnitude of the forecast error — only the probability of an unusually large miss.
- It does not replace or recalibrate the underlying NWP (GEFSv12) forecast. The GEFSv12 forecast itself is the primary product; this system provides a reliability score on top.
- It does not classify the meteorological regime (monsoon depression, western disturbance, etc.) — regime classification is future work.

TRAINING DATA FACTS:
- Geography: Maharashtra sub-region (18–22°N, 72–76°E) at ~0.25° grid resolution.
- Period: 2000–2004 (GEFSv12 reforecast), 44 unique issue dates, 35,200 rows (5 lead times × multiple grid cells × 44 dates).
- Variable: precipitation_sum (mm/day).
- Truth source: IMD 0.25° gridded rainfall analysis (imdlib).
- No synthetic data anywhere in the pipeline.

MODEL PERFORMANCE (test set, chronological holdout, final 15% of issue dates):
- XGBoost (calibrated): ROC-AUC 0.9316, PR-AUC 0.7907, Brier Score 0.0480
- Logistic Regression: ROC-AUC 0.8597, PR-AUC 0.6744
- Climatological Baseline: ROC-AUC 0.4358, PR-AUC 0.1044
- XGBoost outperforms the climatological baseline by +0.4958 ROC-AUC points (a decisive improvement).
